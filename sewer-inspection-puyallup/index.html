Sewer Camera Inspection in Puyallup, WA

A sewer camera inspection shows what is actually inside the pipe. It is the quickest way to tell a root clog from a collapse, to check a home before you buy it, or to confirm that a repair worked.

What a sewer camera inspection shows

A waterproof camera on a flexible rod travels through your sewer line from a cleanout. A monitor shows the inside of the pipe live, and the unit tracks how far it has travelled.

On screen you will see the pipe material, any roots, grease or scale, cracks, joints that have shifted, low spots that hold water, and signs of a collapse. That evidence is what a repair plan should be built on.

Why people schedule one

Before buying a house

A line that looks fine from the outside may hide roots or a sag. A camera check during the inspection window gives you something concrete to negotiate on.

After repeated clogs

If a line keeps backing up, the camera shows whether the cause is buildup, roots or a damaged pipe.

Before digging or landscaping

Locating the line and its depth avoids a hit during a fence, patio or tree project.

After a repair

A follow-up camera run confirms that a lining or replacement is smooth and continuous.

How the inspection goes

  1. 1

    Find access

    A cleanout in the yard, basement or garage is best. Without one, a toilet may be removed to enter.

  2. 2

    Run the camera

    We pass the camera downstream until it reaches the main or the property line.

  3. 3

    Locate and mark

    At each point of interest, a locator reads depth and position above ground.

  4. 4

    Review the findings

    We show you the footage and explain what it means, what is urgent and what can wait.

Not every line allows a camera pass. A collapse, a severe offset or a plugged section may stop the camera, and we will stop there and recommend clearing the line first, as described on the sewer line cleaning page.

What the camera can and cannot tell you

The camera shows

  • Pipe material and condition
  • Roots, scale and grease
  • Cracks, offsets and sags
  • The distance to a problem

The camera does not show

  • The exact legal property line
  • Whether a city main downstream is clear
  • A leak hidden in a line that is not inspected
  • Future damage

It also does not replace a permit or a city sewer record. If you need to know where the city sewer main begins, ask the City of Puyallup, or the district that serves your address if you live outside the city limits.

Older pipe and newer pipe

Puyallup's older blocks near downtown date back to the city's early development, and the pipes under them may be older clay or cast iron. Cameras often find root intrusion at clay joints and scaling inside cast iron.

In South Hill neighborhoods developed more recently, the camera more often shows grease, a sag from settling, or a construction-era issue such as a poorly sealed joint. Either result is useful, because it tells you what to fix.

Codes and references for this page

A camera inspection records condition and does not change the pipe, so it needs no permit. If the camera finds a defect, repair work falls under the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) as amended by Washington. Keep the footage, because a permit office or a buyer may ask for it.
