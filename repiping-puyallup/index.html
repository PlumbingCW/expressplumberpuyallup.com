Whole-House Repiping in Puyallup, WA

Repiping replaces the supply pipes that run through your walls, floors and crawlspace. It is a planned project for homes with galvanized steel, failing copper or repeated leaks.

If a pipe is leaking right now, call the 24/7 line for burst pipe repair first. Repiping is the next step when repairs keep returning.

Signs your home needs repiping

Rusty or brown water

Rust-colored water at first draw suggests corroding galvanized steel or iron pipe.

Low pressure at every fixture

Corrosion narrows galvanized pipe from the inside, so flow drops even when the main is open.

Repeated leaks

More than one leak in a short period means the pipe is failing along its length, not at a single point.

Visible corrosion or green stains

Green crust at copper joints or flaking steel indicates deterioration.

Pinhole leaks in copper

Small sprays or spots that return after repair signal thin pipe walls.

Age of the home

Homes built in the mid 1900s or earlier may have galvanized steel supply lines still in service.

Repair or repipe: how to decide

One leak in otherwise sound pipe is a repair. Several leaks, rusty water and low flow suggest a repipe. We check the pipe at several points and explain what we see. If the pipe is fine, we say so.

Repiping galvanized pipe

Galvanized steel was a standard supply pipe for decades, and it corrodes from the inside. Deposits build up, the opening narrows and pressure falls. Water can come out brown after the house has been unused for a while.

Replacing it opens the whole system and gives you full pressure. It is also the point where a pressure reducing valve, new shutoffs and a new main valve get installed.

How a repipe works

  1. 1

    Walk-through

    We trace the existing lines and plan new routes, mostly through attics, crawlspaces and existing chases.

  2. 2

    Small openings

    Access holes in drywall are cut where needed, and they are patched afterward.

  3. 3

    New pipe and fittings

    New PEX or copper runs from a manifold or main to each fixture.

  4. 4

    Test and restore

    We pressure test the system, flush the lines and close up. Water is restored the same day for most homes.

Most repipes take a few days, depending on the size of the house and how many stories. Water is off for part of each day, and we plan the schedule around your household.

Permits are generally required for a repipe, and rules vary, so ask the City of Puyallup building department or Pierce County for your address. Your plumber can pull the permit when needed.

Partial repiping

Not every home needs a full repipe. A single bathroom, a kitchen or a section of old galvanized between two good sections can be replaced separately. This is often the practical answer when only part of the house has failing pipe, and it can be done in stages.

What affects the cost of repiping

Repiping is one of the larger plumbing projects, and the price is set by the house.

Codes and references for this page

Repiping follows the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) as amended by Washington for pipe materials, sizing, support and testing, and a whole-house repipe normally needs a permit and an inspection. The City of Puyallup handles permits inside city limits and Pierce County handles the rest.

Washington requires a licensed plumbing contractor for plumbing work done for others under RCW 18.106, and the Department of Labor and Industries lets you check a license at lni.wa.gov/verify.
