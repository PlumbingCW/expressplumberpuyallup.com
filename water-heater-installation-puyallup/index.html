Choosing a new water heater

The right unit depends on household size, fixture count, fuel and space. A family of four with two bathrooms usually looks at a larger tank than a couple in a small home, and a house with high simultaneous demand may suit a tankless unit better.

We start with a few questions and a look at what you have now. Gas line size, vent route, panel capacity and floor space all decide what can go where.

What is installed with the heater

Beyond the unit itself, several items are part of a proper installation. A permit tip sheet from the mybuildingpermit.com program, based on the 2015 Uniform Plumbing Code with Washington amendments, lists seismic straps mounted to wall framing within the upper and lower thirds of the tank, and states that plumber's tape is not an acceptable strap.

It also lists an expansion tank on a closed system, a relief valve discharge pipe sloped to drain to an approved location, a shutoff valve on the cold inlet, and on gas units a shutoff valve and sediment trap on the gas line.

Our install checklist follows the same list. We also check that the venting has the right clearance to combustibles, that combustion air is available, and that electric units on a concrete floor or in an unconditioned space have the insulation the sheet calls for.

Items a good install should show

  • !Straps. Two straps, screwed into framing, not into drywall.
  • !Expansion tank. Mounted and charged to match your water pressure.
  • !Relief valve discharge. Piped down to a safe termination, not capped.
  • !Shutoffs. A working valve on the cold inlet, and on gas units a gas shutoff and sediment trap.

Permits and code in Puyallup

Permit rules vary by jurisdiction, so ask the City of Puyallup building department what applies to your address. If you live in unincorporated South Hill, Pierce County rules may apply instead. A permit, when required, is pulled by the contractor, and an inspection may follow.

Washington requires plumbing contract work to be done by a licensed plumbing contractor under chapter 18.106 RCW, and the state's L&I website has a lookup where you can check any plumber's license, workers' comp status and complaint history before you hire.

Hybrid heat pump heaters

A hybrid water heater adds a heat pump to a standard electric tank. It pulls heat from the surrounding air, so it needs open space around it and a room that stays above a minimum temperature.

In a Puyallup garage or utility room, that usually works fine. The unit cools the room slightly, makes a low fan sound and drains condensate that needs a pathway to a drain or pump.

Instant hot water and recirculation

If waiting for hot water is the complaint, a recirculation pump or a small point-of-use unit near the farthest fixture can help more than a bigger tank. The right choice depends on pipe layout and whether there is a return line.

We can look at the layout and tell you which option is realistic. The call is the same line as for any other install.

Planning around Puyallup's wet season

Most rain in Puyallup falls between October and March, and water heater failures often surface in the first cold weeks. Scheduling an install in late summer or early fall puts the work on a weekday and avoids an after-hours replacement.

If your heater is over ten years old or has any leak, a planned install is cheaper in disruption than a failure on a holiday weekend.
