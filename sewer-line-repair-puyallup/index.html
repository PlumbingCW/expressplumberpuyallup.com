Sewer backup emergency: what to do first

A sewer backup shows up in the lowest fixture first. A basement floor drain, a ground-floor shower or a toilet that gurgles when the washer drains are the usual early signs, and they usually mean the main line is blocked beyond your house.

Do not run water to see whether it clears. Each fixture adds to the volume that has nowhere to go, and the next place the water will emerge may be your floor.

While you wait

  • !Stop all water use. No washing, flushing or showering until the line is cleared.
  • !Keep the area clear. Sewage carries bacteria. Keep children and pets out, and wear gloves if you must touch anything.
  • !Do not use chemicals. Drain cleaner will not dissolve a root mass or a broken pipe, and it puts caustic liquid in the work area.
  • !Photograph the damage. Pictures help with an insurance claim if the backup reached the floor.

What a camera shows us before any digging

We feed a small waterproof camera through a cleanout and watch the pipe in real time. Roots, grease, offsets, cracks, sags and collapse all look different on screen, and the camera also records the distance to the problem.

A locator then finds that point above ground, so a repair crew can dig one small hole instead of guessing along the route. This is the step that most often saves a homeowner from an unnecessary trench, and it is covered in more detail on the sewer inspection page.

Lining installs a resin-saturated sleeve inside the old pipe and cures it into a new smooth pipe within the old one. It is a good fit when the pipe is cracked or rooted but still holds its overall shape.

If a section has collapsed or the pipe has sagged and holds water, a lining will not correct the grade, and the section must be replaced by digging.

Cast iron, clay and plastic pipe

The pipe material changes the repair. Older homes may have clay or cast iron sewer pipe, and homes built in more recent decades usually have plastic. Cast iron rusts from the inside and flakes, which narrows the pipe long before it cracks, and it responds well to lining when it is still round.

Clay pipe is made in short lengths with many joints, and roots find the joints. A rooted clay line may need cutting and lining, or replacement of the damaged lengths.

Septic systems and what we do not handle

Most Puyallup addresses connect to a sewer, but some rural parcels use septic. We repair the plumbing between the house and the tank or the sewer main, but we do not pump or repair septic tanks, drain fields or pump chambers. For septic work, a licensed septic service is the right call, and we can help with the house side.

Wet winters, high rivers and sewer backups

Atmospheric-river rains in December 2025 raised the Puyallup River enough to lead to evacuation notices in Puyallup and Sumner, according to local news coverage. Saturated ground and high water tables stress sewer laterals, and a pipe with an existing crack can admit groundwater and soil.

After a high-water event, watch for slow drains and wet spots, and have a camera run on any line that has backed up before. Ask your sewer provider where the owner's responsibility for the line ends and the utility's begins.

Codes and references for this page

Sewer line repairs and replacements follow the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) as amended by Washington for pipe material, slope and cleanouts, and most work beyond a spot repair needs a permit and an inspection. Ask the City of Puyallup, or the sewer provider that serves your address, before digging.
