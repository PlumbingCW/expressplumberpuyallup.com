Disposal humming or jammed

A humming disposal that does not spin has a jam. Something hard such as a bone, a fruit pit or a bottle cap is wedged against the flywheel, and the motor is trying to turn it.

Turn the power off at the switch. Most disposals have a hex socket in the bottom center, and a hex key turned back and forth frees a jam. A red reset button on the bottom restores the unit after it trips.

Safety first

  • !Power off. Switch it off at the wall and unplug the unit if it has a plug.
  • !No hands. Never reach into the chamber. Use tongs or pliers to remove debris.
  • !Do not run it dry for long. Run cold water while grinding.
  • !No chemicals. Drain cleaner damages seals and splashes.

Leaks under the sink: when it is an emergency

Leaks come from three places. The sink flange at the top, the unit's body and the drain connection each leak differently. Water at the top of the unit means a failed flange seal. Water from the side of the unit means a cracked housing, which means replacement.

A leak at the drain pipe or the dishwasher hose is a simple connection fix. Dry the area, run water and watch to identify the drip point before calling.

Slow drain or clog with a disposal

A disposal does not stop grease from coating the drain line downstream. When a sink backs up with a disposal fitted, the clog often sits past the disposal in the trap arm.

We clear the line through the trap or a cleanout, check the dishwasher connection for backflow, and explain which scraps to leave out, such as grease, coffee grounds in quantity, pasta and potato peels.

How long does it take to install a garbage disposal?

A like-for-like replacement typically takes one to two hours. The plumber removes the old unit, cleans the sink flange, sets the new mounting ring, connects the drain and the dishwasher inlet, and tests for leaks.

A first-time installation takes longer because it may need a new electrical outlet or switch under the sink, and a plumber may need an electrician for the wiring. If the sink has a single drain and no trap arm, adding a disposal can also need a modified drain.

Choosing the right size matters. Horsepower, feed type and noise insulation vary. A half-horsepower unit is fine for light use, and a larger unit suits a busy kitchen.

Disposals and hard water in Puyallup kitchens

The City of Puyallup's 2024 report lists average hardness at 89 ppm. Minerals can leave a film inside the disposal and the drain trap, so a regular flush with cold water and a quick cleaning keeps the unit from smelling.

Disposals also increase the load on older drains, which is why homes near downtown with older lines may clog sooner than newer homes.
