Signs of a main line emergency: the sewer is blocked

A single slow sink usually means a local clog. A main line problem shows up in several places at once. The lowest toilet gurgles when the washing machine drains, a tub fills with gray water when you flush, or a floor drain starts to rise.

A sewage smell near a cleanout or a drain, or wet soil above the line in the yard, points to the same thing. These signs are urgent because the blockage can push sewage into the house.

Safe steps while you wait

  • !Stop using water. Every flush and every load adds to the backup.
  • !Open windows if the smell is strong. Sewer gas is unpleasant and can be harmful in an enclosed space.
  • !Do not enter standing sewage. Keep bare skin out and keep children and pets away.
  • !Know your cleanout. Tell the dispatcher where it is, and clear the area around it.

How we clear a main sewer line

Most main lines are cleared through a cleanout with a powered cable that has a cutting head. The head chops roots and breaks up blockages, and the cable pulls debris back toward the cleanout.

After the line runs again, we camera it to check what is left and to learn why the blockage formed. That step is the difference between a line that stays clear and one that backs up again in six months.

For grease and scale, hydro jetting cleans the pipe wall in a way a cable cannot. For a pipe that proves to be cracked, offset or collapsed, cleaning is only a pause, and we will discuss sewer line repair with you.

Sewer drain cleaning and floor drains

Floor drains in garages, basements and laundry rooms connect to the main, and a floor drain that smells or backs up often shares the same blockage. We clear the floor drain with a smaller cable and check the trap for dried seals.

A floor drain that is not used often can dry out and let sewer gas into the room. Pouring a quart of water down it every month keeps the seal in place.

Storm drains and yard drains

Storm drains and yard drains carry rainwater, not sewage, and they clog with leaves, silt and roots. In a wet winter, a clogged yard drain can pool water against a foundation or garage. We clear them with a cable or a jetter and flush them to check the flow. Do not connect a storm drain to the sanitary sewer, which is not allowed in most places.

Rain, roots and the Puyallup valley

Puyallup's rain falls mostly between October and March, and roots search for moisture all year. After a long wet season, roots enter joints in older lines and lawns above shallow lines get greener than the rest.

If your sewer clog repeats every winter, a camera pass and a jetting pass in late summer is cheaper than an after-hours call in January.

Codes and references for this page

Cleaning a sewer line is maintenance, and the line itself falls under the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) as amended by Washington. Where the owner's pipe ends and the utility's begins depends on the provider, so confirm that with the City of Puyallup or your sewer district.

Washington requires a licensed plumbing contractor for plumbing work done for others under RCW 18.106, and the Department of Labor and Industries lets you check a license at lni.wa.gov/verify.
