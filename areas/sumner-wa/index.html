High water first: what Sumner's valley means for your plumbing

Sumner sits in the Puyallup River valley, and the December 2025 atmospheric-river rains showed what that means. Local news reported that Sumner police ordered residents of a mobile home park to evacuate after levee leaks, while the City of Sumner stated that, to its knowledge, no one in the city was displaced by the flooding.

For a homeowner, the practical lesson is simple. Wet ground and high water put stress on sewer laterals and sump areas. After any high-water event, run a slow-drain check, watch floor drains and call a plumber at the first gurgle or smell, because a line that has taken on groundwater can fail later.

Sumner is also in the lahar hazard zone for Mount Rainier, and part of the city sits on the 5,600-year-old Osceola Mudflow, which left a deposit about 300 feet thick. That has no day-to-day effect on a leaking faucet, but it is a reminder to know where your main water valve is and to keep a flashlight and a wrench near it.

Older downtown blocks and newer homes

Sumner was founded in 1853 as Stuck Junction, platted in 1883 and renamed Sumner in 1891. The older streets around downtown and the Sounder commuter rail station have buildings that date back many decades, and older buildings often have older drain and supply pipe.

That is why a camera is the first move on an old line. It shows whether you are looking at roots in clay, scale in cast iron or a sound pipe with a simple clog. Newer homes on the edges of town more often have grease and hair clogs and a sag or two from settling.

Sumner also has a large commercial and distribution base. REI keeps corporate offices and a large distribution center in the city, and the warehouse district draws trucks, staff and heavy restroom use. A business with a floor drain, a grease line or a backflow assembly should know where its main and branch shutoffs are, and we can walk through that on a planned visit.

Sumner facts that matter for your plumbing

Sumner had 10,621 residents at the 2020 census and uses ZIP codes 98390 and 98352. It is served by the Sumner School District, and the city sits in the Puyallup River valley. For water, sewer and flood questions, the City of Sumner is the right source.

The City of Sumner's flooding page directs residents to Pierce County to report damage and lists 211 and county human services for support. If a flood has affected your plumbing, document the damage and call us after you are safe.
