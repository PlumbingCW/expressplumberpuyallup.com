The historic center of Puyallup

Ezra Meeker platted the town in 1877 on part of his farm and named it for the Puyallup people. The city incorporated legally on August 16, 1890, with Meeker as its first mayor. Pioneer Park, which the Meekers gave to the city for a park by 1890, still anchors the district, with the library, a playground and a statue of Meeker.

The Meeker Mansion, an Italianate house finished around 1890, stands nearby. The Washington State Fairgrounds are close too, so downtown fills with visitors for the Daffodil Festival in spring and the fair each September. Heavy event traffic is a real factor for the cafes and shops that rely on their restrooms and kitchens.

Older homes close to the center

Washington's historic preservation plan for Puyallup says the oldest surviving houses date from the 1890s, in Queen Anne, Italianate and vernacular styles. Much of the housing stock from the 1910s to the early 1930s is Craftsman or Craftsman Bungalow. A real estate guide describes the downtown neighborhood as a range of homes, from historic Craftsman houses to new construction.

Age alone does not tell us what is under the floor. Some old houses have had their drains and supply lines replaced, and others have not. That is why we begin with a look, not an assumption.

Why a camera comes first on an older line

A sewer camera shows the pipe material, the joints, any root intrusion and any sag. It turns a guess into a picture you can see on a screen.

If you are buying a house in the older blocks, the same inspection can be done before closing. The sewer inspection page explains the steps.

Permits and the city's own tools

Downtown Puyallup lies inside the city, so permits come from the City of Puyallup. The city runs an online permit portal at permits.puyallupwa.gov, and City Hall is at 333 S. Meridian. Water heater replacement and other permitted plumbing work follows the Uniform Plumbing Code with Washington amendments.

Washington also requires plumbing contractors to be licensed, and you can look up any contractor on the Department of Labor and Industries verify tool before you hire.

Before you call about a downtown leak

  • Shut the main valve if water is flowing, and know where it is before you need it.
  • Move rugs, boxes and electronics away from wet floors.
  • For a restaurant, note which fixture or floor drain is affected and stop using it.
  • Write down when the problem started and what changed.

Codes and references for this page

Plumbing work in downtown Puyallup follows the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) as amended by Washington, with permits issued by the City of Puyallup. Older buildings may have pipe that predates current code, so repairs are made to the code that applies when the work is done.

Washington requires a licensed plumbing contractor for plumbing work done for others under RCW 18.106, and the Department of Labor and Industries lets you check a license at lni.wa.gov/verify.
