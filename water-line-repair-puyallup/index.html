Signs of a water line emergency or a slow leak

A buried line leak does not always surface. It may drop pressure at every tap, make the meter spin with all fixtures off, produce a wet or unusually green patch of yard, or add a steady hiss at the point where the line enters the house.

Muddy or discolored water after a repair or a pressure change is another sign. Any of these merits a call, and a sudden gush or soaked yard is an emergency.

From the meter to the house: who owns what

The meter usually marks where the utility's responsibility ends and yours begins, but the exact boundary differs between utilities. If you are a City of Puyallup water customer, ask the city where its responsibility ends at your address, and if you are served by another district on South Hill, ask that district. We repair and replace the owner's side of the line.

Older lines in galvanized steel corrode from the inside, and copper lines can develop pinholes. Newer lines are often PEX or HDPE. If a line has leaked more than once, replacement is usually the better value.

A leak locator pinpoints the leak so the excavation is small, which is why the leak detection step comes first.

Low water pressure

Low pressure has several causes, and not all of them are underground. A partly closed main valve, a clogged strainer, a failing pressure reducing valve, corroded galvanized pipe inside the house and a leak on the service line all produce weak flow.

We start by measuring static pressure at an outside hose bib. If it is low at the meter side of the house, the line or valve is suspect. If it is normal there and low at fixtures, the problem is inside.

High pressure matters too. Pressure above the recommended range wears fixtures and appliances, and a pressure reducing valve is the usual fix. Inside-house causes of poor flow are covered on repiping when pipes are the culprit.

The shutoff valves that matter

A homeowner main valve inside the house lets you stop the water in an emergency. A valve that has not moved in years may stick or leak when first turned, and replacing a failing main valve is a small job that gives you real control.

We also check the pressure reducing valve and the expansion tank where present, because a failed one can strain the whole system.

Codes and references for this page

Water service lines, from the meter to the house, follow the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) as amended by Washington and the water provider's own rules for the connection at the meter. The City of Puyallup and other providers set who owns which segment, so confirm yours before digging.

Washington requires a licensed plumbing contractor for plumbing work done for others under RCW 18.106, and the Department of Labor and Industries lets you check a license at lni.wa.gov/verify.
