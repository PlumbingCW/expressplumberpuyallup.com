Tankless Water Heater Flush Cost in Puyallup, WA

A tankless water heater flush circulates a mild acid through the heat exchanger to dissolve scale. It is the routine maintenance most manufacturers call for, and skipping it is the usual reason a tankless unit starts showing error codes or heating poorly.

In Puyallup, where the city reports moderately hard water, a regular flush protects a unit that costs far more than the maintenance. This article explains what is involved, what moves the price and how to decide between DIY and a visit.

Written by the Puyallup Express Plumbing team · Published November 14, 2026 · Updated November 14, 2026

What scale does inside a tankless unit

A tankless unit heats water in seconds by passing it through a narrow, hot heat exchanger. Dissolved minerals come out of solution on those hot surfaces and form a hard coating. Over months, that coating narrows the passages and acts as insulation.

The effects are slow and easy to miss. Water takes longer to heat, the unit runs hotter than it should, temperatures swing, and the display shows an error. A flush removes the coating before it causes damage.

How a flush is done

  1. 1

    Shut it down

    Turn off the gas or power and close the isolation valves on the water lines.

  2. 2

    Connect the pump

    Attach hoses from a small pump and a bucket to the service ports on the unit.

  3. 3

    Circulate the solution

    Run white vinegar or a manufacturer-approved descaler through the heat exchanger for the time the manual calls for.

  4. 4

    Rinse and clean the screen

    Flush with clean water, clean the inlet filter and reopen the valves.

  5. 5

    Test

    Fire the unit, check the outlet temperature and look for error codes.

That is the whole job. It takes a plumber an hour or so on a unit with service valves, and longer if the valves are missing or the unit is badly scaled.

DIY flush or a professional visit

A flush is within reach of a handy homeowner. You need a small submersible pump, two hoses with the right fittings, a bucket and a few gallons of vinegar. The unit must have service valves, and you must follow the manual.

The risks are small but real. Closing the wrong valve, leaving the gas on, or skipping the filter cleaning can leave a unit that does not fire correctly. A visit from a plumber includes a check of venting, combustion and error history, which a homeowner flush does not.

How often to flush

Many manufacturers suggest once a year. In very hard water, twice a year may be better. If you have not flushed in years, start with a flush now and watch the result. Our tankless water heater page describes repair and conversion work too.

Signs your tankless unit needs a flush now

Watch for water that takes longer to turn hot, temperatures that swing between hot and cold during a shower, a unit that clicks and shuts down, or an error code on the display. Each of these can come from scale on the heat exchanger or a clogged inlet screen.

Reduced flow at every hot tap while the cold side stays strong is another clue, because the heat exchanger is the narrowest point in the system. If you see any of these, schedule a flush before the unit overheats and a repair becomes a replacement.

A unit that has never been flushed may need more than one pass. Be ready to hear that the first visit removes most of the scale and a second follow-up is wise a few months later.

Doing it yourself versus calling

A handy homeowner can flush a tankless unit with a small pump, two hoses and a bucket of white vinegar, if isolation valves are already installed. Without those valves, a plumber should add them first, because cutting into a live gas appliance is not a casual job.

The risk of doing it yourself is skipping steps. A flush that leaves vinegar in the lines, or one that does not clear the inlet screen, does little good, and a missed error code can hide a larger problem.

What to do before a tankless flush

  • !Shut off power or gas. Follow the unit manual and close the supply.
  • !Let the unit cool. Hot water and a hot exchanger can scald.
  • !Smell gas? Leave. Call the gas utility or 911 from outside.
  • !Call a plumber if unsure. Isolation valves must be present first.

Tankless flushing and Puyallup's water

The City of Puyallup's 2024 water quality report says its water comes mostly from springs, with Salmon Springs the largest source at about 51 percent, plus wells. It lists average hardness of 89 ppm and says iron and manganese are filtered at Well #17.

Moderate hardness means scale builds more slowly than in very hard areas, but it still builds. A yearly flush is a sensible default, and if your unit shows scale-related codes sooner, move to twice a year. Customers of other water systems on South Hill can ask their provider for the same kind of report.
