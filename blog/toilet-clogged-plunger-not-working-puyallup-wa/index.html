Toilet Clogged and the Plunger Is Not Working: What to Do in Puyallup

A toilet that will not clear with a plunger is one of the most common home plumbing problems. Most of these clogs can be solved in minutes with the right technique or the right tool, but a few signal a bigger problem that a plunger will never touch.

This guide covers what to try, what to avoid, and how to tell whether the clog is in the toilet or in your main sewer line. If water is rising toward the rim, close the stop valve behind the toilet first.

Written by the Puyallup Express Plumbing team · Published November 14, 2026 · Updated November 14, 2026

First: stop the overflow

Do not flush again. Lift the tank lid and push the flapper down to stop the tank from refilling. Close the stop valve on the wall by turning it clockwise. Put towels around the base, and then start on the clog.

Try these in order

  1. 1

    Use the right plunger

    A flange plunger, with the extra rubber cone, seals the toilet drain far better than a flat sink plunger.

  2. 2

    Push gently, then pull

    Make a seal and push slowly to avoid splashing, then pull up sharply. Repeat for a minute or two.

  3. 3

    Add hot water and dish soap

    Pour a good squirt of dish soap and a bucket of hot, not boiling, water from waist height. Wait ten minutes.

  4. 4

    Use a closet auger

    A closet auger is a short, curved snake made for toilets. Feed it past the trap and crank it to hook or break up the clog.

  5. 5

    Stop and call

    If none of that works, or other drains are slow too, call a plumber.

Why the plunger fails

A plunger works on soft clogs, such as paper and waste. It fails on solid objects, such as a toy, a razor, a baby wipe wad or a pad, because pushing water at a solid object moves it deeper or wedges it tighter.

It also fails when the plunger is the wrong type, the water level is too low to cover the cup, or the clog is beyond the toilet itself, in the branch line or the main sewer.

Things not to do

  • !Do not use chemical drain cleaner. It can sit in the bowl, damage the porcelain seal and splash when you plunge or auger.
  • !Do not flush repeatedly. Each flush adds water. Wait and let the level fall.
  • !Do not use a wire hanger. It scratches porcelain and can lodge in the trap.
  • !Do not ignore a second fixture backing up. That is a main line sign, not a toilet problem.

Common things people flush that cause clogs

Wipes labeled flushable do not break down like paper. Cotton swabs, dental floss, feminine products, paper towels and hair all catch and build. Small toys and cat litter are also frequent.

Even a toilet with a perfect flush can clog if too much paper goes down at once. Teaching everyone in the house to flush twice for large loads helps.

When to call a plumber

Call when the auger fails, when you suspect a foreign object, when the toilet clogs repeatedly, or when another fixture is affected. We handle toilet repair and the line behind it through drain cleaning. A plumber can pull the toilet, check the flange and use a camera when the clog keeps returning.

Keeping the next clog away

Most toilet clogs are preventable. Keep a small covered bin in the bathroom so wipes, floss and cotton swabs never reach the bowl, and teach children that the toilet is not a trash can.

If your toilet clogs more often than once or twice a year, look at the flush itself. A weak flush can come from clogged rim jets, a low tank water level or a worn flapper, and a plumber can restore the full flush in a short visit. A toilet that flushes weakly clogs more easily, even with ordinary use.

When a clog is not the toilet

If the tub gurgles when you flush, or a sink backs up with the toilet, the blockage is in the branch or the main line, not the bowl. A plunger will not help, and repeated flushing can push sewage onto the floor.

In that case stop using water, and call. A cable or a camera from a cleanout gets to the real blockage, and the earlier you call, the less cleaning you face.

Who is responsible for the sewer in Puyallup

Most Puyallup addresses connect to a public sewer, and the line from your house to the main is generally the owner's responsibility, but the exact boundary depends on the utility. City Hall at 333 S. Meridian handles city utility questions, and unincorporated South Hill addresses may be served by Pierce County or another provider.

If your toilet backs up and a neighbor's does too, call the utility as well as a plumber, because the problem may be in the public main.
