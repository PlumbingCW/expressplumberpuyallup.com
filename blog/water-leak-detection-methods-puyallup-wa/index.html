Water Leak Detection Methods Used in Puyallup, WA Homes

A hidden leak rarely announces itself with a puddle. More often it shows up as a water bill that crept up, a patch of warm floor, a musty smell or a meter that keeps turning. Finding it without tearing out half a wall takes a method, not a guess.

This guide walks through the leak detection methods plumbers use, starting with a test you can do yourself in ten minutes, and explains which method suits a leak in a slab, a wall, a ceiling or the yard.

Written by the Puyallup Express Plumbing team · Published November 14, 2026 · Updated November 14, 2026

Start with the meter test

Before anyone brings equipment, confirm that you have a leak at all. The test costs nothing and takes about ten minutes.

  1. 1

    Shut everything off

    Close all faucets, stop the dishwasher and washing machine and make sure no irrigation zone is running.

  2. 2

    Read the meter

    Find the meter and note the leak indicator or the dial. Take a photo.

  3. 3

    Wait

    Leave the water off for fifteen to thirty minutes without using any fixture.

  4. 4

    Read again

    If the dial or indicator moved, water is escaping somewhere between the meter and your fixtures.

Check toilets first, since a toilet flapper is the most common silent leak. Add a few drops of food coloring to each tank and wait fifteen minutes without flushing. Color in the bowl means a worn flapper.

The methods plumbers use

MethodHow it worksBest forLimit
Pressure testIsolate a line, pressurize it and watch the gaugeTelling which line is leakingDoes not pinpoint the spot
Acoustic listeningGround microphones and contact sensors hear escaping waterPressurized metal pipe, slab leaksPlastic and very small leaks are quiet
Thermal imagingInfrared camera shows temperature differencesHot-water leaks, wet insulationSurface temperature must change
Moisture meterProbes measure moisture in drywall and woodWalls, floors and ceilingsFinds dampness, not the pipe
Drain cameraA camera on a rod inside the drain lineCracks, offsets, standing water in drainsNeeds access and flow
Tracer gasA harmless gas pumped into a sealed line escapes at the leakSmall leaks when other methods failRequires a line that can be isolated

No single method works every time, which is why a good detection visit uses two or three. A pressure test narrows the leak to a line, listening finds a zone, and thermal imaging or a moisture meter confirms the spot.

The result is an opening in the wall or floor about the size of your hand, not a trench across the house.

Matching the method to the leak

Slab leak

Warm floor spots and a hissing sound point to a pressurized line under concrete. Listening gear and thermal imaging are the main tools.

Wall or ceiling leak

A moisture meter traces the damp path back toward the source, and running fixtures one at a time shows which line feeds it.

Yard leak

A soft, green or sunken patch near the meter box or along the line suggests a buried service line leak, found with listening gear and a locator.

Drain leak

A stain below a bathroom with no supply leak points to a drain, tested with a camera or a controlled fill-and-watch.

Irrigation leak

A zone-by-zone pressure test finds the cracked valve or broken head that adds to a high bill.

Intermittent leak

A leak that runs only when the water heater cycles or a sprinkler zone fires needs logging on the meter.

What detection does not do

Detection finds the leak. It does not fix it, and it does not dry out the damage. A plumber will repair the pipe, and a restoration company handles extraction and drying.

Detection also cannot find a leak that is not currently running or one in a line that is impossible to isolate. When that happens, we say so and recommend what to monitor.

Meters and bills in Puyallup

If you are a City of Puyallup water customer, your meter and bill come from the city utility, and City Hall at 333 S. Meridian can tell you how to read it. If you are on a different district on South Hill, ask your own provider where your meter sits.

The city's 2024 water quality report describes more than 200 miles of pipeline. Because the city's system has been built over many decades, the age and the material of the pipe between the meter and your house can differ greatly from one street to the next.
