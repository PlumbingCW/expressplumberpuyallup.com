How to Install a Toilet Gasket: What Puyallup Homes Need to Know

A toilet gasket, or wax ring, is the seal between the base of the toilet and the drain flange in the floor. When it fails, you get water around the base, a sewer smell or a toilet that rocks. Replacing it is a manageable job, but it often reveals other problems, such as a damaged flange or a soft floor.

This guide walks through the replacement step by step, flags the mistakes that cause the seal to fail again, and tells you when to stop.

Written by the Puyallup Express Plumbing team · Published November 14, 2026 · Updated November 14, 2026

Signs the gasket has failed

Water pooling at the base after a flush, a wet spot on the floor near the toilet, a sewer smell, a toilet that rocks, and stained flooring beneath are all signs. The seal can fail from age, from a loose toilet or from a flange that sits too low.

Do not rely on caulk. Caulk around the base hides a leak and can trap water against the floor.

What you need

  • A new wax ring or a wax-free gasket sized for your drain
  • New closet bolts and washers, since old ones corrode
  • A putty knife, rags and a bucket
  • A wrench and a screwdriver
  • Disposable gloves
  • A flange repair ring if the flange is cracked or low

Steps

  1. 1

    Shut off and drain

    Close the stop valve, flush, and sponge out the remaining water from the tank and bowl.

  2. 2

    Disconnect

    Remove the supply line and the nuts on the closet bolts.

  3. 3

    Lift the toilet

    Rock it gently to break the seal, lift straight up and set it on a towel or cardboard.

  4. 4

    Clean the flange

    Scrape off old wax, check the flange for cracks and plug the drain with a rag to block sewer gas.

  5. 5

    Set the new seal

    Place the new gasket on the flange or on the toilet horn, and fit new bolts.

  6. 6

    Lower and tighten

    Lower the toilet straight down without twisting, press firmly, then tighten the nuts evenly and alternately.

  7. 7

    Reconnect and test

    Attach the supply line, open the stop valve, flush several times and check for leaks.

Common mistakes

  • !Twisting the toilet as you set it. That can squeeze the wax ring out of position.
  • !Overtightening the bolts. Porcelain cracks. Tighten evenly, a little at a time.
  • !Reusing the old wax ring. It does not reseal properly.
  • !Skipping the leak test. Flush three or four times and check the base.

Wax or wax-free gasket

A wax ring is inexpensive and adapts to the flange, but it can only be used once and can be messy. A wax-free gasket is a rubber or foam seal that can be reused and works better on a flange that sits slightly high or low.

Either works when installed correctly. Many plumbers prefer a reinforced wax or a rubber gasket with a sleeve for a floor that sits higher than the flange.

When to stop and call a plumber

If the floor is soft, if the flange is badly corroded, if you see water or damage beneath the toilet, or if the toilet still leaks after a new seal, stop. These are signs of a larger problem that a plumber should look at. How to tell the toilet is set right

After the install, sit on the closed lid and rock gently. The toilet should not move at all. Run a bead of caulk around the front and sides, leaving a small gap at the back so a future leak can show.

Check the floor beside the toilet after a few flushes and again the next day. A dry floor means the seal is holding, and a damp one means you should lift it again before the leak hurts the subfloor.

What to do if the toilet leaks again

  • !Shut the stop valve. Turn the valve at the wall clockwise to stop the supply.
  • !Keep the bowl from overflowing. Do not flush until the leak is found.
  • !Mop up and ventilate. Standing water under a toilet softens the floor.
  • !Call a plumber. Call if the floor feels soft or sewage is visible.
