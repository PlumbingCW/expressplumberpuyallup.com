How Long Does It Take to Install a Garbage Disposal in Puyallup, WA?

A straight swap of one garbage disposal for another usually takes one to two hours. A first-time installation, where there was no disposal before, can take half a day or more.

The difference comes from the sink, the wiring and the drain. This guide walks through the steps, the surprises that add time and what you can do ahead to keep the visit short.

Written by the Puyallup Express Plumbing team · Published November 14, 2026 · Updated November 14, 2026

Steps in a swap

  1. 1

    Power off

    Switch off the breaker and unplug or disconnect the old unit.

  2. 2

    Remove the old unit

    Disconnect the drain, the dishwasher hose and the mounting ring, and lower the unit away.

  3. 3

    Prepare the sink

    Clean the old putty or gasket from the flange and check the sink opening for cracks.

  4. 4

    Mount the new unit

    Seat the flange, attach the mounting assembly and lift the disposal into place.

  5. 5

    Connect and test

    Reconnect the drain and dishwasher hose, restore power, then run water and the disposal to check for leaks.

What makes a disposal install take longer

No outlet under the sink

A disposal needs power. A new outlet and switch means electrical work and possibly an electrician.

A single-bowl sink or an odd drain

The trap arm may need to be re-routed to line up with the disposal outlet.

Dishwasher knockout plug

New disposals have a plug in the dishwasher inlet. It must be removed with a hammer and screwdriver, or the dishwasher will not drain. This is a classic miss.

An old sink flange that will not release

Years of putty and corrosion can fuse the flange to the sink.

A cracked or loose sink

Weak or cracked stainless or composite sinks need attention before a heavy unit hangs from them.

Existing clogs

A disposal cannot fix a drain that is already slow. We clear the line first.

Prepare before the plumber arrives

  • !Clear the cabinet. Empty the cabinet under the sink and lay a towel on the floor.
  • !Know the power source. Tell us if there is an outlet or a wall switch for the disposal.
  • !Choose the unit. Have a model in mind, or ask for a recommendation by horsepower and noise.
  • !Fix slow drains first. A clog downstream will make the new unit back up.

Choosing a disposal

Horsepower, feed type and sound insulation are the choices that matter. A half-horsepower unit handles light cooking for one or two people. A larger unit grinds tougher scraps and runs more quietly in a busy kitchen.

Continuous feed units run while you push scraps through. Batch feed units run only with the stopper in place and are safer where children are around.

Care that prevents the next call

Run cold water before, during and for about fifteen seconds after grinding, so fats stay solid and move down the line. Cut large scraps into smaller pieces and feed them slowly.

Avoid grease, bones, fibrous vegetables and large amounts of coffee grounds or starchy food such as pasta and rice. If the unit smells, grind a handful of ice and citrus peel, then flush with cold water. A disposal does not replace the trash can, and treating it gently is the cheapest way to avoid a service visit.

What slows an install down

The most common delay is a trap or drain that does not line up with the new unit. Older sinks may need a new trap arm, and a double bowl sink with a dishwasher connection takes a few extra fittings.

A second delay is electrical. A disposal needs an outlet under the sink or a hardwired connection to a switch, and a plumber cannot always add one. If there is no power, a licensed electrician may need to run it, so plan for that when you schedule.

Disposals and permits in Puyallup

Replacing a disposal is minor work, but permit rules vary by jurisdiction and by whether wiring is involved. Ask the City of Puyallup building department, or Pierce County if you are on South Hill, what applies to your address. Wiring work in Washington may require an electrician with the right license.

The City of Puyallup's 2024 water quality report lists moderately hard water at 89 ppm, which leaves a mineral film in the grinding chamber over time. A quick rinse of cold water after each use keeps the unit fresher.
