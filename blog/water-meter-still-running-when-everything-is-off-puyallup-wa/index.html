Water Meter Still Running When Everything Is Off? A Puyallup, WA Guide

If the meter keeps moving after every tap is closed, water is going somewhere. It might be a toilet that leaks silently, an irrigation valve that sticks open, a leak in the line between the meter and your house or a hidden leak inside a wall or slab.

The good news is that you can narrow it down with a few simple checks. This guide walks through them in order of likelihood so you know what to fix yourself and when to call.

Written by the Puyallup Express Plumbing team · Published November 14, 2026 · Updated November 14, 2026

Confirm it is real

  1. 1

    Turn everything off

    Stop the dishwasher, washer, ice maker and irrigation. Close all taps.

  2. 2

    Find the meter

    It is usually in a box near the street or at the property line. Lift the lid carefully.

  3. 3

    Look at the indicator

    Most meters have a small leak indicator, a triangle or a star, that spins when any water flows.

  4. 4

    Wait and compare

    Note the reading, wait fifteen minutes with no use, and read again.

Some meters show a slow creep when a water heater refills or an ice maker cycles. If the movement is steady, you have a leak.

The main valve test

Close the main shutoff at the house and watch the meter. If the meter stops, the leak is inside the house or at an irrigation connection after the shutoff. If it keeps moving with the main closed, the leak is in the line between the meter and the house.

This one test splits the problem in half. A leak in the yard line is outside, and you may see a wet or sunken patch of ground, or hear water in the pipe near the meter.

If you cannot find it in an hour

  • !Keep the main closed. Open it only when you need water.
  • !Photograph the meter. Record the reading and time for your utility and insurer.
  • !Call a plumber. Describe what you tested and the results.
  • !Ask your utility. Some utilities investigate unusual usage or have adjustment policies. Ask yours.

What to expect from a plumber

A plumber repeats the tests, pressure tests the lines and uses listening gear to locate the leak. A yard leak is often fixed with a small excavation and a repair or a short replacement section. An interior leak may need an opening in a wall or floor.

Fix the leak first, then talk to your utility about the bill. Never wait on a running meter, since the water wastes money and a hidden leak can damage your home for weeks.

Leaks that only run sometimes

Not every leak runs all day. A sprinkler zone that leaks only when it fires, a water heater relief valve that opens when the tank heats, or a toilet that refills a few times an hour can all hide from a quick check.

If the meter test comes back clean but your bill is still high, write down the reading each morning and each night for a week. Patterns show up quickly. A jump overnight points inside the house, and a jump at the sprinkler time points to irrigation.

What not to do

Do not tamper with the meter or the shutoff on the utility's side. That equipment belongs to the utility, and altering it can bring fines.

Do not ignore a small movement because the bill looks fine this month. Leaks grow, and a pinhole can become a burst. Checking now costs ten minutes, and waiting can cost a ceiling.
