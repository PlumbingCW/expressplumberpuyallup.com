Water Softener Installation Guide for Puyallup, WA Homes

Once you have decided a softener is worth it, the installation is where a good result is made or lost. A unit in the wrong place, without a bypass or with a poor drain line causes years of small headaches.

This guide explains the pieces of a softener install, what the plumbing involves and what to check at the end of the visit.

Written by the Puyallup Express Plumbing team · Published November 14, 2026 · Updated November 14, 2026

Where a softener goes

The usual spot is on the main water line just after the meter or the main shutoff, so every fixture in the house gets treated water. It needs a nearby floor drain or standpipe, a power outlet, and a flat, dry place for the tank and the brine tank.

Garages and basements are common. In a home with a crawlspace, access and freeze protection need thought. A plumber should also keep an untreated cold tap outside for the garden, because softened water is not ideal for lawns.

What the installation involves

  1. 1

    Shut off and drain

    Close the main valve and open a tap to relieve pressure.

  2. 2

    Cut in

    Cut the main line and install the inlet and outlet connections with a bypass.

  3. 3

    Run the drain

    Connect the drain line to a standpipe or floor drain with an air gap.

  4. 4

    Add the brine line and overflow

    Connect the brine tank overflow to a safe drain.

  5. 5

    Power and program

    Plug in the controller and set hardness, time and regeneration.

  6. 6

    Flush and test

    Run the unit through a regeneration, check for leaks and test the water.

Common mistakes

No bypass valve

Without one, any service means shutting off the whole house.

Drain without an air gap

Wastewater could be pulled back into the unit.

Hot water line treated first

Install on the cold supply or the main line, not only on the hot side.

Wrong hardness setting

The controller needs your actual hardness, not a default.

Tank placed on soft ground

A full brine tank is heavy, so set it on a firm, level base.

Permits and code

Treatment equipment that ties into the water supply and drain is usually plumbing work. Whether a permit is needed depends on the work and the jurisdiction. Ask the City of Puyallup permit office, or Pierce County if you are in unincorporated South Hill, and note that the state requires plumbing work for others to be done by a licensed plumbing contractor. You can check any contractor at the Washington State Department of Labor and Industries verify tool.

After the install

  • !Ask for a walk-through. Learn where the bypass, salt fill and controller are.
  • !Check the water. Hardness should drop to near zero at a tap after the first regeneration.
  • !Fill the salt. Use the kind the manufacturer recommends.
  • !Watch for leaks. Check connections after a day and again after a week.

Salt, regeneration and what to expect day to day

A softener regenerates on a schedule, usually overnight, flushing brine through the resin to recharge it. During that cycle the house may have a short period of untreated water, so avoid running laundry at that hour.

The brine tank needs salt every month or two, depending on household size and hardness. Check it when you do other household checks, and keep the lid on to avoid dust and moisture. If the salt forms a hard crust or bridge, break it up with a broom handle so the unit keeps dissolving salt.

Softener or something else

If your main complaint is taste or chlorine, a softener will not fix it. A carbon filter solves that problem, and the two can be installed together. Some households put a small drinking water filter at the kitchen tap and a softener on the main line.

If you are on well water, have it tested first. Iron, manganese and bacteria need different treatment, and a softener alone can be damaged by high iron.

Questions to ask your plumber before the visit

Ask where the unit will sit, how the drain will be run and whether a bypass is included. Ask which pipe material connects the unit, and whether the old water lines need work first.

Ask what happens if the main shutoff does not close fully. Older valves often fail when turned for the first time in years, and replacing one is easier before the day of the install.

Licensing and local rules

Washington requires a licensed plumbing contractor for plumbing work done for others, under RCW 18.106, since July 1, 2021. The Department of Labor and Industries lets you verify a license, bond and insurance online at lni.wa.gov/verify before you hire.

The City of Puyallup handles permits for addresses in the city, and permits.puyallupwa.gov is the city's permit portal. Addresses outside the city limits follow Pierce County.
