Toilet overflowing: what to do right now

In this order

  • !Close the stop valve. It is on the wall or floor behind the toilet. Turn it clockwise until it stops.
  • !Hold the flapper. Lift the tank lid and press the rubber flapper down to keep the tank from refilling the bowl.
  • !Do not flush again. Each flush adds water to the bowl.
  • !Put towels down and turn off power nearby. Water and bathroom outlets are a risk, so switch off the breaker if the floor is flooded.

Once the water stops, a plunger sometimes works. If it does not, a toilet auger reaches past the trap. If other fixtures are also draining slowly, the clog is past the toilet and in the branch or the main line.

That distinction matters because pushing harder on a main line clog only moves the problem.

When a toilet clogs and the plunger fails

A toilet has a built-in trap shaped like an S, and wipes, paper towels, toys and heavy paper all catch there. Most clogs clear with a flange plunger and steady pressure, and a closet auger pulls out what remains.

If a toilet clogs repeatedly, or if a second toilet or the tub also gurgles, we check the drain line. The cause may be roots or buildup that drain cleaning or sewer line cleaning solves.

Leaks at the base of the toilet

Water around the base of a toilet usually means the wax ring or gasket has failed, the closet bolts are loose, or the flange is damaged. Rocking in the seat is a sign the flange is not holding the toilet firmly.

A new wax ring or a modern gasket replaces the seal, and a flange repair ring fixes a broken or low flange. Leaving a leak at the base soaks the subfloor, so it deserves a quick repair.

Running toilets and silent leaks

A toilet that runs can waste a lot of water, and a silent leak from tank to bowl can add to a high bill without making a sound. Put a few drops of food coloring in the tank and wait 15 minutes without flushing. If color shows in the bowl, the flapper leaks.

Running toilets and unexplained bills are a common first sign of a larger problem on the leak detection page, so rule out the toilet before looking for a hidden leak.

Toilet installation and replacement

Replacing a toilet means closing the water, draining the tank and bowl, removing the old toilet, checking the flange, setting a new seal and leveling the new unit. The details matter, such as the rough-in distance, the flange height and the floor condition.

If you are installing a gasket, a quick method is to center the gasket on the flange, lower the toilet straight down, and seat it without twisting. We also replace supply lines and stop valves at the same visit, since they are the most likely to leak next.

Hard water and toilet parts in Puyallup

The City of Puyallup's 2024 report lists average hardness at 89 ppm, and notes that iron and manganese are filtered at Well #17. Minerals can leave deposits in the rim jets and fill valve, which reduces flush strength over time.

If your toilet flush weakens, clean the rim holes with a mirror and a small brush first. If that does not help, a plumber can replace the fill valve and flapper at the same visit.

Codes and references for this page

Toilet installation and repair follow the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) as amended by Washington, including fixture clearances and the flange connection to the drain. Like-for-like repairs are minor work, while moving a toilet changes drain piping and may need a permit.

Washington requires a licensed plumbing contractor for plumbing work done for others under RCW 18.106, and the Department of Labor and Industries lets you check a license at lni.wa.gov/verify.
