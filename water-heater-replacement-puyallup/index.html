When a failed tank is an emergency

A tank that leaks from its shell will keep leaking, and fifty gallons can reach a finished floor quickly. Cold water on the other side of a wall, rusty water and a rumbling that gets worse each week are the earlier warnings.

If you are standing in water, shut the cold supply valve, cut power at the breaker or turn the gas control off, and call. We will schedule a replacement for the first open window.

Repair or replace: a quick way to decide

The decision comes down to age, location of the leak and what has already been repaired. A short rule of thumb helps before the plumber arrives.

Choosing the replacement type

Like-for-like tank swaps are the fastest because the gas line, vent, power and water connections already exist. If the old tank is gas, the new one is usually gas, and if it is electric, we check the circuit and wire size against the new unit's label.

Moving to a hybrid heat pump unit takes more room and a condensate drain, but it uses far less electricity. Moving to a tankless unit needs a larger gas line or a high-amp circuit, so it is better as a planned job through water heater installation than as a same-night swap.

What a replacement visit includes

The plumber drains and disconnects the old tank, hauls it out, and sets the new unit on a proper pan or base. Connections are made with new flexible lines or rigid piping, and the relief valve discharge is run to an approved location.

A permit tip sheet from the mybuildingpermit.com program, based on the 2015 Uniform Plumbing Code with Washington amendments, lists the details a replacement is expected to meet. These include seismic straps within the upper and lower thirds of the tank, an expansion tank on a closed system, a relief valve discharge six to twenty-four inches above grade where it terminates outside, and a shutoff valve on the incoming cold line.

Permit rules for a replacement vary by jurisdiction, so we recommend you ask the City of Puyallup building department, or Pierce County if you live in unincorporated South Hill, what applies to your address. Permits can be pulled by the contractor when required.

Signs the tank is near the end

Rust-colored hot water, pooling around the base, a sulfur or metallic smell, and a tank older than about a decade.

Signs the problem is elsewhere

Hot water that runs out fast can be a failed element, and low hot-water pressure can be a clogged aerator or valve.

Why failed tanks cluster in the wet months

Puyallup's cool, wet winters put extra demand on water heaters, and a unit that has been limping along all summer is often the one to quit in the first cold stretch. Sediment is also heavier in tanks that have never been drained.

If your heater is more than ten years old, a planned swap on a weekday costs less in disruption than a failure at night. You can book it through the same line.

Ask your utility before you size a tank

The City of Puyallup's 2024 water quality report says its water is treated with chlorine and averages 89 ppm hardness. That is moderately hard by common scales, so sediment will accumulate in a tank over time, and a flush every year or so helps.

If your home gets water from a different provider, such as a South Hill water district, ask that provider for its own water quality report and test your tap, because hardness differs by source.
