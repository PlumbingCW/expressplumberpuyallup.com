When a clogged drain is an emergency in Puyallup

A slow sink is an annoyance. A drain that gurgles, bubbles in a second fixture or pushes water up into the tub is a blockage deep enough to flood a bathroom, and it will not fix itself overnight.

Call any hour if water is rising toward the rim of a fixture, if a basement or crawlspace floor drain is backing up, or if you smell sewage inside the house. Those are the signs that the blockage sits in a branch or the main line instead of a single trap.

Do this while you wait for the plumber

  • !Stop the water. Pause the dishwasher, washing machine and showers. Every gallon you add has to go somewhere.
  • !Leave the toilet alone. Flushing into a blocked line sends the water to the lowest open fixture, often a tub or floor drain.
  • !Skip the chemicals. Caustic cleaner that sits in a clog splashes back on the person who runs the cable and can damage older pipe.
  • !Move belongings. Lift rugs, boxes and electronics off any floor near a backing-up drain.

What actually blocks drains in Puyallup homes

Most calls trace back to a short list of causes. Hair and soap film build up in tub and shower lines, cooking grease hardens in kitchen lines, and wipes or paper towels catch in toilet branches.

The City of Puyallup's 2024 water quality report lists its water at an average of 89 ppm hardness and notes that hard water does not dissolve soap readily. In practice that means soap residue clings to pipe walls and thickens a hair clog faster, so a tub that drains slowly every few months is worth a cable pass before it stops completely.

In older homes around downtown Puyallup, where the townsite was platted in 1877 and the city incorporated in 1890, the house drain is often an older material with rough joints that catch debris. Tree roots find those joints, and a root-filled line will clog again within months unless the roots are cut back.

Newer South Hill neighborhoods, many built after the area's rapid growth in the 1990s, tend to clog for simpler reasons: grease, hair and flushed wipes. We confirm which one it is before we choose a tool.

Kitchen sink clogs and grease

The kitchen sink is the single most common drain call because the pipe under it carries grease, starch and food solids together. A garbage disposal grinds food finely, but it does not stop grease from cooling and coating the line downstream.

We cable the sink line past the trap arm, flush it with hot water to check the flow, and look at what comes back on the cable. If the line keeps clogging every few weeks, it usually means a belly or heavy buildup further along, and we recommend a hydro jetting pass or a camera look instead of repeating the same cable.

Snaking, cabling and jetting: how we choose

Snaking and cabling are the same idea at different sizes. A small hand-fed snake handles a sink or tub, while a drum-driven cable with a cutting head handles a three-inch or four-inch branch line.

If the cable pulls back clean but the drain still runs slow, the pipe walls are likely coated. That is the point where hydro jetting makes sense, and where a short camera run through sewer inspection shows whether the pipe is sound enough to take it.

A leak in a drain line is a quieter version of the same problem. Water that disappears from a crawlspace or stains a ceiling below a bathroom can be a cracked drain joint rather than a supply leak, and a camera or a controlled water test tells the two apart.

Codes and references for this page

Drain, waste and vent work follows the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) as amended by Washington, which covers traps, vents and cleanouts. Clearing a clog rarely needs a permit, but moving or adding a drain usually does, so ask the City of Puyallup or Pierce County first.

Washington requires a licensed plumbing contractor for plumbing work done for others under RCW 18.106, and the Department of Labor and Industries lets you check a license at lni.wa.gov/verify.
