When a faucet or valve is an emergency

A dripping faucet can wait for a daytime visit. A supply line that is spraying, a stop valve that will not close or a handle that comes off in your hand cannot.

Shut the water at the fixture's stop valve, or at the main if that fails. Then place a bucket and towels under the leak, and call.

Quick steps

  • !Find the stop valve. Under a sink, two small oval valves feed the faucet. Turn them clockwise.
  • !If the valve will not turn, stop. Forcing an old stop valve can snap it and open the line. Close the main.
  • !Open the faucet. That relieves pressure and drains the line.
  • !Shut off the main for a tub or shower valve. Those valves often have no local shutoff.

Kitchen and bath faucet repair

Most faucet leaks come from worn cartridges, seats or O-rings, and from mineral deposits. A cartridge faucet drips because the cartridge wears. A compression faucet drips from a worn washer.

In a home with moderately hard water, such as the 89 ppm average the City of Puyallup reports, scale can lock a cartridge or clog an aerator. Soaking the aerator in vinegar and replacing a worn cartridge often restores flow.

Stop valves and broken supply lines

A supply line or stop valve that bursts under a sink is a clear emergency, and it is a common cause of cabinet floods. Old braided lines can corrode and fail, and old stop valves can seize or leak when finally moved.

Replacing stop valves and supply lines at the same visit as a faucet job is the best value, since those parts are the next failure point. If the line has failed inside the wall, we treat it as pipe repair.

Shower, tub and showerhead leaks

A showerhead drip is a minor fix. A leak inside the shower valve body, or water showing behind the wall, calls for a closer look. The valve may need a new cartridge or a complete replacement.

Tub spouts and diverters fail too. If water comes from the tub spout while the shower is on, the diverter is worn and a replacement spout solves it.

A tub or shower that drains slowly is a drain problem rather than a faucet problem. We clear the line and check the stopper and strainer, and the details are on the drain cleaning page.

Sink repair and replacement

Sink problems include loose basins, leaking drains and failing traps. We replace faucets, strainers, traps and tailpieces, and we reseal sink basins. If a sink is being replaced as part of a remodel, the plumbing installation page covers rough-in and fixture setting.

Fixtures in Puyallup's older and newer homes

Puyallup's older homes near downtown often have older fixtures and stop valves that have not moved in decades, while South Hill homes built since the 1990s are more likely to have newer valves, though many of them are builder-grade.

If you are remodeling a kitchen or bath, replace the old stop valves and supply lines at the same time, since they are cheap and hard to reach later.

Codes and references for this page

Fixture work follows the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) as amended by Washington, which includes requirements for stop valves, air gaps and backflow protection at certain fixtures. A like-for-like faucet swap rarely needs a permit. Adding or relocating supply or drain lines usually does.

Washington requires a licensed plumbing contractor for plumbing work done for others under RCW 18.106, and the Department of Labor and Industries lets you check a license at lni.wa.gov/verify.
