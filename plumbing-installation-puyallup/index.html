What we install

Plumbing installation includes sinks, faucets, toilets, showers and tubs, laundry connections, hose bibs, new water lines and drains, and water treatment equipment. It also includes the rough-in work done inside walls before drywall.

We work with homeowners, remodelers and builders. A good install starts with a plan for where lines run, where the shutoffs go and how drains will slope.

Shower plumbing rough-in

Rough-in is the plumbing that goes into the wall before the shower is finished. It includes the valve at the right height, the supply line to the showerhead and tub spout, the drain at the correct location, and blocking in the wall to support fixtures.

The details that matter are the valve depth relative to the finished wall, the drain size and slope, and a pressure test before the wall is closed. Once tile is on, mistakes are expensive.

Water softeners and filtration

The City of Puyallup's 2024 water quality report lists an average of 89 ppm hardness. That is moderately hard, so a softener is a choice, not a necessity, and some households prefer to live with it. If you do see scale on fixtures or want softer water for laundry, a softener or conditioner may suit you.

Because hardness differs between water systems, we recommend that you test your own tap and ask your provider for its report before buying equipment. A softener needs a drain, power, a place for salt and a bypass valve.

New construction and additions

For new construction or additions, plumbing happens in phases. First comes the underground or under-slab drain, then rough-in in the framing, then top-out and trim after finishes. Each phase may need an inspection.

We coordinate with the builder on the schedule so the plumbing does not hold up the next trade.

Backflow prevention and hose bibs

Outdoor hose bibs and irrigation connections need protection against backflow. Vacuum breakers and assemblies keep irrigation or hose water from siphoning back into the house supply. Some assemblies need periodic testing, and a certified tester does that work. Ask your water provider what applies to your address.

Permits and your address

Permit rules differ between the City of Puyallup, Pierce County for unincorporated South Hill, and other jurisdictions. The City has an online permit portal at permits.puyallupwa.gov, and City Hall is at 333 S. Meridian.

Ask the building department what applies before you start. Washington also requires plumbing contract work to be done by a licensed plumbing contractor, and you can check any plumber on the state's L&I verify tool.

Codes and references for this page

New plumbing, bathroom rough-in and treatment equipment follow the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) as amended by Washington, with inspections at rough-in and at final. Permits are issued by the City of Puyallup, or by Pierce County for unincorporated South Hill addresses.

Washington requires a licensed plumbing contractor for plumbing work done for others under RCW 18.106, and the Department of Labor and Industries lets you check a license at lni.wa.gov/verify.
