Emergency plumbing services in Puyallup

Every service below is available through the same 24/7 line. Emergency calls are handled first, and planned work is scheduled on weekdays. Each page explains what to do while you wait, how the repair works and what drives the price.

Drains and sewers

A slow sink starts with drain cleaning, and a clog that keeps returning moves to hydro jetting. When several drains are slow at once, sewer line cleaning clears the main, and a camera on the sewer inspection page shows whether the line needs sewer line repair.

Water heaters

A failed tank is handled through emergency water heater replacement, and a unit with a fixable fault through water heater repair. Planned swaps are on the water heater installation page, and on-demand units are on the tankless water heater page.

Leaks and pipes

A running meter or a stained ceiling starts with leak detection. Water spraying from a pipe is a burst pipe repair call, and a buried supply line is covered on the water line repair page. Houses with failing galvanized pipe may need repiping.

Fixtures, toilets and disposals

Overflowing or leaking toilets are on the toilet repair page, and dripping faucets and failed shower valves are on faucet and sink repair. Jams and leaks at the sink are handled through garbage disposal repair.

Commercial and installation

Shops, restaurants and offices can read about commercial plumbing, including grease traps and backflow testing. Remodels, shower rough-in and water treatment are on the plumbing installation page.

Codes and references for this page

Every service listed here is performed under the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) as amended by Washington. Permit needs depend on the work and the address, and the City of Puyallup permit portal at permits.puyallupwa.gov is the place to start.

Washington requires a licensed plumbing contractor for plumbing work done for others under RCW 18.106, and the Department of Labor and Industries lets you check a license at lni.wa.gov/verify.
