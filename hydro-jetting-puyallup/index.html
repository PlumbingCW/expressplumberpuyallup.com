Why drains clog again, and when it becomes an emergency

A cable pushes through the middle of a clog and leaves the coating on the pipe walls. Over weeks, new grease, hair and paper catch on that coating and the opening narrows to the same blockage you just paid to clear.

If you have cleared the same line more than twice in a year, or you notice slow drains, gurgling and sulfur smells returning after each cable visit, the walls are the problem and not the clog.

How hydro jetting works

A jetting machine sends high-pressure water through a hose fitted with a nozzle. Water jets point backward to pull the hose forward and forward to cut at blockage, so the pipe is scoured as the hose moves along it.

The result is a pipe wall washed down to bare material instead of a narrow channel through the middle. It clears grease, soap scum, silt and scale, and with a root-cutting nozzle it can clear fine root hair as well.

Before the plumber arrives

  • !Stop adding water. Hold off on laundry, dishwashing and showers.
  • !Find the cleanout. A capped pipe in the yard, garage wall or basement is where the jetter usually enters. Clear the path to it.
  • !Tell us what runs slowly. Which fixtures gurgle or back up first tells us where the blockage sits.
  • !Do not use chemical cleaner. Standing cleaner can splash back during jetting.

When jetting is the wrong tool

Jetting is not for every pipe. Before we use it on a main line we run a camera to check the pipe's condition, because high-pressure water can worsen a pipe that is already cracked, offset or collapsed.

In those cases we point you toward sewer line repair or lining instead, and use cabling to open the line safely first. That camera pass is explained on the sewer inspection page.

Jetting suits

  • Recurring grease or soap-scale clogs
  • Roots in a sound clay or concrete pipe
  • Kitchen and restaurant lines
  • Preventive cleaning of a line with a history

Skip or modify jetting for

  • Collapsed or heavily offset pipe
  • Badly corroded old cast iron
  • A new clog that a cable can clear
  • Pipe with an unknown path and no cleanout

Kitchen lines, main lines and commercial lines

Kitchen lines in houses and restaurants collect grease that coats the pipe and slowly closes it from the sides. A jetting pass followed by a camera check shows whether the line is clean or whether a low spot is holding water.

On main lines, jetting is often scheduled after a camera shows scale or root hair that a cable cannot fully remove. Food-service buildings often schedule jetting on a regular cycle, which is covered on the commercial plumbing page.

What hard water has to do with it

The City of Puyallup's 2024 water quality report lists an average hardness of 89 ppm. Mineral deposits and soap residue build slowly on pipe walls under those conditions, which is one reason older drains narrow even without grease. Ask your plumber to look at the pipe wall on camera before and after jetting so you can see the difference.

Codes and references for this page

Hydro jetting is a cleaning method and not a code item, but the pipe it cleans falls under the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) as amended by Washington. Inspect the line by camera first on older pipe, since high pressure can worsen cracks. If a repair follows, ask the permit office whether it needs a permit.
